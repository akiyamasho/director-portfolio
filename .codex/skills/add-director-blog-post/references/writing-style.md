# Director journal writing style

## Voice

- Start with the actual shoot, test, edit, drawing, artifact, or production problem.
- Write as a director speaking to a knowledgeable colleague who did not see the
  work happen.
- Use verified concrete nouns: shot, lens, stage, model, version, duration,
  location, constraint, or production term.
- Move between one specific example and the larger directing workflow.
- Keep warmth and candid opinion without manufacturing drama or certainty.
- End with a real next step or the last useful observation, not a generic CTA.

## Rhythm

- Mix sentence and paragraph lengths.
- Use headings only when they help navigate substantial material.
- Use lists for real inventories, stages, or results, not marketing cadence.
- Connect paragraphs through subject matter rather than canned transitions.
- Avoid a forced introduction, recap, conclusion, or minimum length.

## Hard bans

Do not use em dashes or semicolons in authored prose. Do not use these phrases or
close formulaic variants:

- `It is important to note that`
- `But here's the catch`
- `This underscores the importance of`
- `In today's fast-paced world`
- `In conclusion`
- `To summarize`
- `Let's delve into`
- `At its core`
- `game-changer`
- `uncharted waters`
- `embark on a journey`

Avoid slogan-like contrast templates such as `X isn't the problem, Y is` and
`not just X, but Y`. Prefer the actual observation.

Use words such as `leverage`, `optimize`, `enhance`, `synergy`, `pivotal`,
`groundbreaking`, `cutting-edge`, `innovative`, and `robust` no more than twice in
total unless they are factual proper names or quotations. Usually replace them
with the real action: draw, shoot, test, cut, compare, render, review, or publish.

## Evidence and bilingual checks

- Support every claim with the input folder, repository, or an approved source.
- Preserve uncertainty and omit unsupported numbers.
- Never invent strategy, obstacles, reactions, quotes, dates, or credits.
- Write Japanese as Japanese. Announce the completed action first, use restrained
  `です・ます`, natural subject omission, and established production vocabulary.
- Keep technical density and candidness in both languages without explanatory
  overtranslation.
- Confirm EN and JA match in claims, media, links, credits, and next step.
